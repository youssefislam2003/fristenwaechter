Fristenwächter

A compliance engine for German commercial fleets. It tracks HU/AU, UVV and Führerscheinkontrolle deadlines — and when a license check fails, it locks the driver and alerts the owner with guaranteed delivery, because letting that person drive is a criminal offense for the Geschäftsführer (§ 21 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 StVG, Halterhaftung).

DPP·1

The problem being solved

Every German company vehicle owner (Halter) is personally responsible for three recurring legal obligations. Missing them isn't an admin inconvenience — it's fines, voided insurance, and in the license case, criminal exposure for whoever runs the company.

ObligationLegal basisCadenceConsequence if missed
HU/AU (TÜV)§ 29 StVZO24 mo (first PKW: 36 mo) — month-granular PlaketteFines, insurance disputes after accidents, vehicle inspection orders
UVV-PrüfungDGUV V70 § 57at least every 12 mo per vehicle/machineBerufsgenossenschaft liability, voided coverage on workplace accidents
Führerscheinkontrolle§ 21 StVGbest practice: every 6 mo per driverCriminal liability of the Geschäftsführer if an unlicensed employee drives

Target customer: Handwerksbetriebe and small fleets, 5–50 vehicles — companies with real exposure and no fleet department. Today they track this in a wall calendar, if at all.

DPP·2

Product scope & positioning

A deliberately narrow B2B SaaS: deadline tracking, attested check logging, automated reminders (90/30/7/0 days, e-mail + SMS), and one exceptional flow — the hard escalation when a license check fails. Two commitments define the product against every competitor:

CommitmentWhat it means in practice
Keine FührerscheinkopienWe never store scans or license numbers. We store the legally useful artifact instead: a tamper-evident log of who checked whom, when, how, result. This makes us a clean Art. 28 DSGVO Auftragsverarbeiter, keeps the product cookie-banner-free, and is a marketing weapon with privacy-sensitive German SMEs.
ZustellgarantieThe § 21 StVG alert cannot be silently lost — not by a crash, a mail-server outage, or a deploy. Architecture, not best effort (see PP·4).
DPP·3

System architecture

Boring on purpose: one encrypted German VPS, one PostgreSQL, two processes from one image. Python 3.11 · FastAPI · SQLAlchemy 2.0 async · APScheduler. No queues, no Kubernetes — the workload is hundreds of events per day, and every moving part removed is an outage that can't happen.

Dual database roles: migration_admin owns the schema; the runtime roles hold explicit per-table grants only — no DELETE, no TRUNCATE, no DDL anywhere. Row-level security isolates tenants below the application layer.

DPP·4

The critical path: a failed license check

Employee cannot present a valid license at the biannual check. From this moment, letting them drive is a crime the owner commits. The system's response, end to end:

┌── one database transaction · zero network i/o ──┐
  1. Hard lockDriver is flagged not authorized to drive — guarded by optimistic version locking, so racing writes fail loudly (HTTP 409) instead of silently.
  2. Evidence appendedThe failed check joins the driver's SHA-256 hash chain. The row can never be edited or deleted — a database trigger forbids it, and the app role holds no such privilege anyway.
  3. Escalation case openedDeadline: end of the 7th Berlin calendar day. A partial unique index makes a duplicate open case a database impossibility.
  4. Alert intent committedCRITICAL e-mail + SMS to every owner/manager written to a transactional outbox — the intent to notify is exactly as durable as the lock itself.
└── commit — all four effects, or none ──┘
  1. Relay deliversEvery 15 s: claim via FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED (critical first), send, retry on 0s→1m→5m→30m→2h backoff. Exhausted retries dead-letter and page the operator — an undeliverable § 21 alert is treated as an incident, not a log line.
  2. Daily pressure, then breachOpen cases nag the owner every morning at 06:00 Berlin. Past the deadline: status BREACHED, strongest wording.
  3. ResolutionA passed re-check unlocks the driver and closes the case — logged in the same immutable chain, which is precisely the evidence a Geschäftsführer shows if the Staatsanwaltschaft ever asks.

DPP·6

DSGVO posture

AspectPosition
RolesCustomer = Verantwortlicher for driver data; we = Auftragsverarbeiter (Art. 28). The AVV we offer at signup is a sales feature, not paperwork — without it the customer is himself non-compliant.
Data minimizationDriver record = name, contact, license classes, check history. Nothing else can be stored: the API rejects unknown fields, the schema has no columns for them.
Erasure vs. evidenceArt. 17 anonymizes the person; the check chain references only an internal ID plus PII-free booleans, so the customer's liability evidence survives erasure intact. That is the design's central trick.
SubprocessorsHetzner (DE) · Brevo (EU) · seven.io (DE). All-EU: no Schrems-II discussion, ever. No Google Fonts, no third-party assets — this very document loads zero external resources.
RetentionConfigurable per customer (default 36 months after employment end), executed by a nightly purge job — a policy that runs itself.
